#### Why it is still rejected — three reasons, the third structural

1. **COVID lockdown collection.** Fieldwork 7–14 April and 7–11 May 2020, during Spain's
   confinement. Authors' own caveat, verbatim: *"the sample was recruited during the COVID-19
   pandemic and thus, their isolation scores could be inflated by the sense of threat and the
   confinement associated to this pandemic."* Inflated norms flatter the user — the same
   DIRECTION of error as the HRS 57-85 norm this app already rejected (`benchmarks.js:418-421`).
2. **Non-probability online quota panel, N=1,951.** Against CLS's probability-based 160,755.
3. **The percentile map is not invertible, and this is not fixable by better data.** Going
   score → percentile, a raw 3 lands anywhere in p1–p30; a raw 4 in p40–p50. Assigning one
   number means interpolating inside a flat region — the prohibited move, identical to the
   objection that blocks the CLS bands (`benchmarks.js:721`, round-4 lines 107-108).

**Therefore the round-4 conclusion is upgraded.** It is not "no usable UCLA-3 norm has been
found yet." It is: **UCLA-3 cannot support an honest percentile in principle.** Seven possible
values, ~72% of the population inside three of them. A perfect, representative, present-day
Thai UCLA-3 norm would produce the same step function. Stop searching for one.

# Research task: a rankable social-connection norm for working-age adults

## Your role and the single most important rule

You are sourcing numeric population statistics that will be written into a health
self-assessment app used by real people. Every number you return will be **independently
re-verified against its primary source before use**. A number I cannot trace and confirm is
worse than no number, because it costs review time and then gets discarded anyway.

**"NOT FOUND" is a correct, valuable, and expected answer.** The app currently refuses to rank
this aspect and tells the user so; that disclosure is a shipped, deliberate feature, not a bug
I am desperate to fix. A prior deep-research pass returned NOT FOUND on four questions and that
was a successful outcome. **Do not fill gaps.**

## Read this first: what killed the last candidate, and the test it implies

A previous search found a genuine, exact-instrument, working-age, general-population
**percentile table** for the 3-item UCLA loneliness scale (Trucharte et al. 2021, Spanish
version, N=1951, ages 18-75, Table 6, percentiles by age band and sex). It was still rejected,
because the scale has only seven possible values and roughly three-quarters of respondents sit
in the bottom three of them. In the published table a raw score of 3 spans percentiles 1
through 30. Converting a user's score into a rank would mean picking a number inside that flat
region — which is guessing.

**So there is a RESOLUTION TEST every candidate must pass, and it is new:**

> For a norm to be usable, a person's raw score must map to a percentile RANGE no wider than
> about 15 points across most of the scale. State, for every distribution you report, the
> widest percentile span occupied by any single raw score. If one score covers 30+ percentile
> points, say so explicitly and mark the figure **FAILS RESOLUTION**.

Report figures that fail this test anyway — I want the negative result documented — but label
them. Do not present a failing figure as an answer.

**Corollary: do not spend effort on the 3-item UCLA scale.** It has been searched twice and is
now ruled out on structural grounds, not availability grounds. If you happen across a UCLA-3
distribution, report it in one line for the record and move on. The priority is instruments
with **enough score points to rank**.

## Absolute prohibitions

Do not do any of the following, even if it would produce a more complete-looking answer:
- Do not **pool, average, or meta-analyse** figures across studies or countries.
- Do not **interpolate or extrapolate** across age bands or score points.
- Do not **convert between measures** — not between instruments, not between a prevalence and
  a distribution, not between response scales of different lengths.
- Do not **re-band** data into groups the source did not publish. Report the source's own bands
  verbatim, whatever they are.
- Do not **reconstruct a distribution from a mean and SD.** These scales are discrete and
  skewed; a normal fit is exactly the error this app removed once already.
- Do not cite a review, blog, secondary summary, news article, AI answer, or a figure quoted in
  another paper's background section. Go to the **primary publication or official statistical
  release**. If you can only reach a secondary mention, mark it `SECONDARY ONLY — unverified`.

## Known traps in this exact subject area

Real errors already caught in this project:

- **The same instrument in a different response scale.** A Hong Kong UCLA-3 study (N=1653)
  re-coded items 0–3 instead of 1–3. A seven-country pilot used a 4-point scale. **Always
  report the number of response options, their exact labels, and the numeric coding.**
- **Different constructs treated as interchangeable.** "Feels lonely" (direct question),
  "scores high on UCLA-3" (indirect scale), "lacks social support" (Gallup), "is socially
  isolated" (LSNS-6), "lives alone" (census) are five different measurements. Never merge them.
- **A "population" norm that is a clinical or convenience sample.** Hospital, clinic, student,
  single-workplace, single-province, and **disaster-affected** samples are not general-
  population norms. The Japanese TMM cohort is post-earthquake — label frames like this.
- **A "Thai" figure that is not Thai.** A widely cited "Thai" self-efficacy norm traces to 135
  head nurses in Yunnan, CHINA. Confirm the sample's actual country and occupation, not the
  authors' affiliation.
- **Pandemic-era fieldwork.** Loneliness norms collected during 2020–2021 lockdowns are
  inflated and the authors often say so. **Always report data-collection dates**, and flag
  anything collected under confinement.
- **Reverse scoring.** LSNS-6 and UCLA both have higher=worse and higher=better versions.
  State direction explicitly, every time.
